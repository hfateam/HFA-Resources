Help Families Find Your Classes

A practical guide to optimizing your listings for charter schools and ESA families

"There isn't a public formula or guaranteed ranking system. It's not alphabetical order, and it's not one magic keyword. The goal is a listing that's easy for families to find, easy to understand, and easy to say yes to."

The Optimization Framework
1

Know what actually matters

No one outside the platform knows the exact ranking algorithm — and there's no evidence listings are sorted alphabetically. Visibility is generally shaped by:

  • Clear class titles
  • Relevant keywords
  • Complete descriptions
  • Accurate age/grade levels
  • Correct subject categories
  • High-quality images
  • Strong enrollment performance over time
Relevance + engagement — not alphabetical order
2

Study the top listings

Instead of guessing, compare your listing to the most popular classes in your subject. What words show up in their titles? How do they describe the class? What age ranges do they target, and what subjects do they select? You're not copying — you're identifying patterns families already respond to.

3

Optimize your title

A strong title answers three questions fast: what is it, who is it for, and why should someone click? Clear, descriptive titles using words families naturally search for tend to outperform vague or clever ones.

4

Use natural keywords

Think like a parent searching — STEM, coding, reading, math, piano, Spanish, kindergarten, middle school. Work those terms naturally into your title, description, and learning objectives. Write for people first; don't keyword-stuff.

5

Make your description easy to scan

Parents decide quickly. Break one giant paragraph into sections: what students will learn, who the class is for, skills they'll build, materials needed, and what makes your class unique. The easier it is to read, the easier it is to understand.

6

Choose the right categories

Double-check subject, grade level, age range, and delivery format. Even a great class can miss searches if these settings don't match what families are looking for — platforms commonly use them as eligibility filters before ranking ever comes into play.

7

Keep improving

Optimization isn't one-and-done. Every few weeks: refresh titles and descriptions, sharpen keywords, compare against top-performing listings, and watch which classes are getting the most interest. Small, steady improvements tend to outperform one big rewrite.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Vague or overly clever titles that don't say what the class actually is
  • One dense paragraph instead of a scannable description
  • Defaulting to "All Ages" when the class really serves a specific range
  • Skipping images, or using a generic stock photo
  • Publishing once and never revisiting the listing
  • Copying a competitor's title or description word-for-word

Run This Self-Audit Before You Publish

  • My title says what it is, who it's for, and why someone should click
  • I used the words a parent would actually search, not just program jargon
  • My age/grade range reflects who the class is really for
  • My subject category is the closest possible match, not just a convenient one
  • My description is broken into scannable sections, not one block of text
  • I have at least one clear, high-quality image
  • I've looked at this listing in the last 30 days

Key Takeaways

  • There's no publicly documented ranking formula, and no evidence listings are ranked alphabetically.
  • Focus on making your class clear, searchable, easy to understand, and easy to enroll in.
  • Learn from successful listings while keeping your own class authentic.

"Instead of trying to beat an algorithm, focus on creating the best possible listing for families. Clear titles, relevant keywords, complete information, and regular updates give your classes the best opportunity to be discovered and enrolled."
